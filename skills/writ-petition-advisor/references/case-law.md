# Writ jurisdiction — controlling decisions by issue

One-line holdings, not quotations. Confirm each on [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/) by title before citing; neutral citations marked (verify) are from memory.

## Which Article

- **Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras**, AIR 1950 SC 124 — Art. 32 is itself a guaranteed right; the Supreme Court cannot refuse to entertain a petition for enforcement of fundamental rights.
- **P.N. Kumar v. Municipal Corporation of Delhi**, (1987) 4 SCC 609 — the Supreme Court may relegate Art. 32 petitioners to the High Court under Art. 226.
- **Surya Dev Rai v. Ram Chander Rai**, (2003) 6 SCC 675 — scope of certiorari and Art. 227 against subordinate courts; *overruled in part* by **Radhey Shyam v. Chhabi Nath**, (2015) 5 SCC 423 — no writ under Art. 226 lies against the judicial orders of a civil court; the remedy is Art. 227.
- **Waryam Singh v. Amarnath**, AIR 1954 SC 215; **Shalini Shyam Shetty v. Rajendra Shankar Patil**, (2010) 8 SCC 329 — Art. 227 is supervisory, sparingly used, not an appeal.

## Article 12 — "State" and public duty

- **Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib Sehravardi**, (1981) 1 SCC 722 — tests for an instrumentality or agency of the State (share capital, financial assistance, monopoly, deep and pervasive control, public functions, transfer of a government department).
- **Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. Indian Institute of Chemical Biology**, (2002) 5 SCC 111 (7 judges) — the tests are cumulative: control must be functional, financial and administrative, deep and pervasive, not merely regulatory.
- **Zee Telefilms Ltd v. Union of India**, (2005) 4 SCC 649 — BCCI is not "State", but a writ under Art. 226 lies against it in respect of its public functions.
- **Andi Mukta Sadguru Trust v. V.R. Rudani**, (1989) 2 SCC 691 — mandamus against a private aided college to enforce a public duty (payment of statutory dues to teachers).
- **Binny Ltd v. V. Sadasivan**, (2005) 6 SCC 657 — writ against a private body only where it performs a public duty; private employment is outside.
- **Janet Jeyapaul v. SRM University**, (2015) 16 SCC 530 — a deemed university performs a public function; writ maintainable.
- **St. Mary's Education Society v. Rajendra Prasad Bhargava**, 2022 INSC 1180 (verify) — a private unaided school's dismissal of a teacher is a private contractual matter; writ not maintainable absent a statutory duty.
- **Ramakrishna Mission v. Kago Kunya**, (2019) 16 SCC 303 — a charitable hospital is not State; service dispute not amenable to writ.

## The five writs

- *Habeas corpus*: **Kanu Sanyal v. District Magistrate, Darjeeling**, (1973) 2 SCC 674 — production of the body is not essential; legality of detention is the question. **Sunil Batra (II) v. Delhi Administration**, (1980) 3 SCC 488 — anyone may move; letters treated as petitions. **Ichhu Devi Choraria v. Union of India**, (1980) 4 SCC 531 — the detaining authority must justify; burden is on the State.
- *Mandamus*: **Saraswati Industrial Syndicate v. Union of India**, (1974) 2 SCC 630 — a demand and a refusal (express or implied) are ordinarily required. **Comptroller & Auditor General v. K.S. Jagannathan**, (1986) 2 SCC 679 — mandamus may direct the manner of exercising discretion where it has been abused, but does not substitute the court's view. **Union of India v. S.B. Vohra**, (2004) 2 SCC 150 — no mandamus to enact or frame rules.
- *Certiorari*: **Hari Vishnu Kamath v. Ahmad Ishaque**, AIR 1955 SC 233 — grounds: want or excess of jurisdiction, error of law apparent on the face, violation of natural justice; not an appeal on facts. **Syed Yakoob v. K.S. Radhakrishnan**, AIR 1964 SC 477 — error must be self-evident, not one requiring long argument.
- *Prohibition*: **S. Govinda Menon v. Union of India**, AIR 1967 SC 1274 — lies while proceedings are pending before an authority acting without jurisdiction.
- *Quo warranto*: **University of Mysore v. C.D. Govinda Rao**, AIR 1965 SC 491 — a public office of a substantive character created by statute/Constitution; the holder must show title. **Rajesh Awasthi v. Nand Lal Jaiswal**, (2013) 1 SCC 501 — standing is wide; the inquiry is into eligibility, not suitability. **B. Srinivasa Reddy v. Karnataka Urban Water Supply**, (2006) 11 SCC 731 — locus and limits.

## Alternative remedy

- **Thansingh Nathmal v. Superintendent of Taxes**, AIR 1964 SC 1419 — the High Court does not act as a court of appeal where the statute provides a complete machinery.
- **Whirlpool Corporation v. Registrar of Trade Marks**, (1998) 8 SCC 1 — alternative remedy is a rule of discretion, not of jurisdiction; exceptions: enforcement of fundamental rights, violation of natural justice, order wholly without jurisdiction, challenge to vires.
- **Harbanslal Sahnia v. Indian Oil Corporation**, (2003) 2 SCC 107 — reiterates the exceptions.
- **Radha Krishan Industries v. State of Himachal Pradesh**, 2021 INSC 314 (verify) / (2021) 6 SCC 771 — in GST provisional-attachment matters, the exceptions applied; collates the principles.
- **Godrej Sara Lee Ltd v. Excise and Taxation Officer**, 2023 INSC 92 (verify) — the High Court should not dismiss at the threshold where the vires or jurisdiction is in issue; "entertainability" vs "maintainability".
- **Assistant Commissioner (CT) LTU v. Glaxo Smith Kline Consumer Health Care Ltd**, (2020) 19 SCC 681 — a writ cannot be used to circumvent a statutory limitation that has expired on the appeal.
- **PHR Invent Educational Society v. UCO Bank**, 2024 INSC 297 — in SARFAESI/recovery matters the High Court ordinarily must not entertain a writ when the statutory remedy (DRT) exists; lists the narrow exceptions and holds that none applied; strong restatement of self-restraint.
- **United Bank of India v. Satyawati Tondon**, (2010) 8 SCC 110 — the same in financial-recovery matters; interim orders in such writs discouraged.
- **ABL International Ltd v. Export Credit Guarantee Corporation**, (2004) 3 SCC 553 — a writ may lie against the State even in contractual matters where the action is arbitrary or unfair and no disputed facts need trial; the discretion is narrow.
- **Joshi Technologies v. Union of India**, (2015) 7 SCC 728 — contractual disputes involving disputed facts are not for writs.

## Tribunals and service matters

- **L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India**, (1997) 3 SCC 261 (7 judges) — tribunals under Arts. 323A/323B are courts of first instance; their orders are reviewable by a Division Bench of the High Court under Arts. 226/227; direct Art. 32 petitions against tribunal orders are discouraged.
- **Union of India v. Major General Shri Kant Sharma**, (2015) 6 SCC 773 — Armed Forces Tribunal matters: the High Court should not entertain writs where appeal to the Supreme Court lies.
- **Rojer Mathew v. South Indian Bank**, 2019 INSC 1292 — tribunal framework; relevant where the tribunal's constitution is challenged.

## Territorial jurisdiction (Art. 226(2))

- **Kusum Ingots & Alloys Ltd v. Union of India**, (2004) 6 SCC 254 — a part of the cause of action must arise within the High Court's territory; the seat of the authority alone may not suffice; the High Court may decline on forum conveniens.
- **Nawal Kishore Sharma v. Union of India**, (2014) 9 SCC 329 — receipt of the impugned order and its consequences at the petitioner's place can be part of the cause of action.
- **Ambica Industries v. CCE**, (2007) 6 SCC 769 — the "situs of the appellate authority" problem; the Court cautioned against forum shopping.

## Delay, locus, PIL, res judicata

- **Tilokchand Motichand v. H.B. Munshi**, (1969) 1 SCC 110 — delay bars even an Art. 32 petition in the Court's discretion.
- **Chennai Metropolitan Water Supply & Sewerage Board v. T.T. Murali Babu**, (2014) 4 SCC 108 — unexplained delay defeats the discretionary remedy; equities in favour of third parties matter.
- **S.P. Gupta v. Union of India**, 1981 Supp SCC 87 — liberalised locus; PIL by public-spirited persons for those unable to approach the court.
- **State of Uttaranchal v. Balwant Singh Chaufal**, (2010) 3 SCC 402 — PIL filters: credentials, bona fides, prior representation, no private/political motive; directions to High Courts to frame PIL rules; exemplary costs for abuse.
- **Daryao v. State of U.P.**, AIR 1961 SC 1457 — res judicata applies to writ petitions; a decision under Art. 226 on merits bars Art. 32 on the same cause.
- **Direct Recruit Class II Engineering Officers' Assn v. State of Maharashtra**, (1990) 2 SCC 715 — constructive res judicata applies to writs.

## Interim relief and conduct

- **Dalpat Kumar v. Prahlad Singh**, (1992) 1 SCC 719 — prima facie case, balance of convenience, irreparable injury (applied in writ interim orders).
- **Assistant Collector of Central Excise v. Dunlop India Ltd**, (1985) 1 SCC 260 — restraint in staying revenue recoveries; interim orders should not be granted as a matter of course.
- **S.P. Chengalvaraya Naidu v. Jagannath**, (1994) 1 SCC 1 — suppression/fraud on the court disentitles relief.
- **Prestige Lights Ltd v. SBI**, (2007) 8 SCC 449 — a petitioner who conceals material facts forfeits the discretionary remedy.

## Supreme Court routes

- **Art. 136 (SLP)**: discretionary; Supreme Court Rules, 2013, Order XXI — 90 days from the impugned judgment/order (60 days from refusal of a certificate under Arts. 132–134; verify the current Order and the time for certified copies); **Mathai @ Joby v. George**, (2016) 7 SCC 700 — SLP is not a routine appeal.
- **Art. 32 against High Court decisions**: not maintainable as a substitute for an SLP — **Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar v. State of Maharashtra**, AIR 1967 SC 1.
- **Transfer and curative**: **Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra**, (2002) 4 SCC 388 — curative petition after review, narrow grounds.
