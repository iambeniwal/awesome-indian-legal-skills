# Writ petition — structure and the items that vary by court

Formats are prescribed by each High Court's Writ Rules / Rules of Practice and by the Supreme Court Rules, 2013. The skeleton below is the common denominator; **verify the specific court's rules, forms, affidavit format, page margins/font, court fee and e-filing requirements before finalising**.

## High Court — Article 226 (and 227) petition

1. **Cover / cause title** — "In the High Court of [name] at [seat] · [Ordinary Original / Civil Writ / Writ-C etc.] Jurisdiction · Writ Petition (Civil/Criminal) No. ___ of 20__ · [Petitioner] v. [Respondent(s)] · Petition under Article 226 [and 227] of the Constitution of India". Some courts require a "subject" line and the nature of relief (certiorari/mandamus) on the cover.
2. **Index** with page numbers; **urgency application** if sought (many courts require a separate "urgent listing" form).
3. **Synopsis and list of dates** — one page synopsis in plain prose (who, what order, why illegal, what relief), then a reverse-chronological or chronological list of dates with the annexure reference for each event. Several High Courts and the Supreme Court require this; others do not but it never hurts.
4. **Memo of parties** — petitioner(s) with full address, email, mobile; respondents: the State/UoI through the Secretary of the department, the authority that passed the impugned order (by designation), any body whose order is challenged, and every person who benefits from the impugned order (necessary party). Add the contesting private party where relief affects them.
5. **Petition body**
   - Preliminary: petitioner's description and standing; "the petitioner has not filed any other petition on the same cause of action before this or any other court" (mandatory declaration in most courts); territorial jurisdiction paragraph (what part of the cause of action arose within the court's territory).
   - Facts, numbered, each tied to an annexure ("Annexure P-1").
   - The impugned order/action and why it is challenged.
   - Alternative remedy paragraph: either "no alternative efficacious remedy exists" or the exception relied on, with the decision.
   - Delay paragraph where any gap exists.
   - **Grounds**, lettered A, B, C…: each a self-contained proposition — the legal rule, the decision supporting it, and its application to the facts. Keep each to a paragraph; lead with the strongest (jurisdiction / natural justice / vires), then merits.
   - Prayer: main reliefs numbered (writ of certiorari quashing order dated __; writ of mandamus directing respondent no. __ to __ within __ weeks; declaration; costs; "any other order"), then interim prayer separately (stay of operation of the order; status quo; no coercive steps; direction to consider representation), with the equity for each.
6. **Affidavit** in verification by the petitioner (or authorised signatory with the authority annexed), in the court's prescribed form (some courts require a notarised affidavit; some accept e-affidavits).
7. **Interim application** (CM/IA) as a separate document where the rules require it.
8. **Annexures**: impugned order, representations and replies, statutory provisions relied on if unusual, documents proving the right; each with a one-line annexure description page.
9. **Vakalatnama**, memo of appearance, advocate's identity/enrolment, proof of service/notice to the Government Pleader where the rules require advance copy (several High Courts require advance notice to the Standing Counsel for the State/Union before listing).
10. **Court fee** per the State's Court Fees Act / High Court rules (fixed fee for writs in most States — verify the figure) and e-filing fee.

## Supreme Court — Article 32 petition (Supreme Court Rules, 2013, Order XXXVIII)

- Statement that a fundamental right is infringed and which one; why the Supreme Court is approached directly rather than the High Court (national importance, multiple States, earlier directions of the Court, pure question of law).
- Synopsis and list of dates (mandatory), petition in the prescribed form, affidavit, annexures, and an application for interim relief; registry scrutiny for defects before numbering.
- For an SLP (Art. 136), use Order XXI: limitation 90 days (verify), application for condonation of delay if late, certified copy of the impugned judgment (or application to exempt), and the specific "question of law" formulation.

## PIL additions

- Petitioner's credentials affidavit (background, no personal interest, source of information, prior representation to the authority) as the High Court's PIL rules require after *Balwant Singh Chaufal*.
- Disclosure of funding and of any related litigation.
- Reliefs framed in public terms (directions, monitoring, compliance reports), not private gain.
- Be prepared for costs if the court finds the PIL motivated or a private dispute in disguise.

## High Court-specific items to verify every time

| Item | Why it varies |
|---|---|
| Bench/seat allocation (district → seat) | Bombay (Mumbai/Nagpur/Aurangabad/Goa), Allahabad/Lucknow, Madras/Madurai, Karnataka (Bengaluru/Dharwad/Kalaburagi), Punjab & Haryana (one seat but roster), Calcutta (Port Blair/Jalpaiguri), etc. |
| Writ Rules and forms | Each court has its own; some mandate synopsis, some mandate "statement of facts" headings |
| Affidavit format and attestation | Oath commissioner / notary / e-affidavit |
| Advance copy to State counsel | Required before listing in several courts |
| Court fee | State Court Fees Act; different for Art. 226 and 227 in some States |
| E-filing | Mandatory in most courts; file naming, bookmarking and OCR requirements |
| Urgent mentioning | Mentioning slot and form; some courts use an "urgency memo" |
| PIL rules | Credentials affidavit; cost deposit in some courts |
| Division Bench vs Single Judge | Roster: service, tax, PIL, habeas, Art. 227 differ by court |
