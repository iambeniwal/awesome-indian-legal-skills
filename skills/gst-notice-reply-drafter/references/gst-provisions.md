# GST notices — provisions, timelines and the decisions that matter

Section numbers are from the CGST Act, 2017; every State GST Act mirrors them with the same numbering. Verify on [cbic-gst.gov.in](https://www.cbic-gst.gov.in/) (Act, Rules, notifications, circulars) and [gst.gov.in](https://www.gst.gov.in/) (portal forms) and record the date checked. Figures marked (verify) were taken from secondary reports.

## 2. Limitation for demands (count from the annual-return due date for the FY, or from the erroneous refund)

| Provision | Periods | SCN by | Order by | Penalty on settlement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s.73 (no fraud) | up to FY 2023-24 | 3 months before the order limit (= due date + 33 months) | due date + 3 years | nil if paid with interest before SCN or within 30 days of SCN (s.73(5),(8)); 10% of tax or ₹10,000 after (s.73(9)) |
| s.74 (fraud, wilful misstatement, suppression) | up to FY 2023-24 | 6 months before the order limit (= due date + 54 months) | due date + 5 years | 15% before SCN; 25% within 30 days of SCN; 50% within 30 days of order; else 100% (s.74(5),(8),(11)) |
| s.74A (common provision; inserted by Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 from 1 Nov 2024, Not. 17/2024-CT) | FY 2024-25 onwards | due date + 42 months | 12 months from SCN, extendable by 6 months by a JC-or-above with reasons (verify) | non-fraud: nil if paid within 60 days of SCN; fraud: 15% before SCN, 25% within 60 days of SCN, 50% within 60 days of order (verify sub-sections) |

Annual-return (GSTR-9) due dates (verify — extended by notification in several years): FY 2017-18: 5/7 Feb 2020; FY 2018-19: 31 Dec 2020; FY 2019-20: 31 Mar 2021; FY 2020-21: 28 Feb 2022; FY 2021-22: 31 Dec 2022; FY 2022-23: 31 Dec 2023; FY 2023-24: 31 Dec 2024; FY 2024-25: 31 Dec 2025. The s.73 order deadlines for FY 2017-18, 2018-19 and 2019-20 were extended under s.168A (Notifications 9/2023-CT and 56/2023-CT) to 31 Dec 2023, 30 Apr 2024 and 31 Aug 2024; the validity of 56/2023 was challenged in several High Courts — check the current position before conceding a demand that relies on it.

Threshold objections that win cases: an SCN issued after the last date is without jurisdiction; an order issued after the limit is void (s.75(10)); s.74 invoked without any specific allegation of fraud/suppression in the SCN cannot be sustained (and the 5-year limit falls away); DRC-01A skipped is a curable irregularity in most High Courts but worth pleading; a DRC-01 that is only a summary (no detailed SCN with reasons) is defective; an order travelling beyond the SCN's grounds is barred by s.75(7); no personal hearing before an adverse order breaches s.75(4) (several High Courts have set such orders aside); adjournments are capped at three (s.75(5)).

## 3. Input tax credit — the usual battleground

- s.16(2): conditions — tax invoice/debit note; receipt of goods/services; tax actually paid to Government by the supplier (s.16(2)(c)); return filed; invoice appearing in GSTR-2B (s.16(2)(aa), from 1 Jan 2022); payment to supplier within 180 days (second proviso); no ITC on depreciation-claimed tax component (s.16(3)).
- s.16(4): time limit — 30 November following the FY (from FY 2021-22; earlier the September return due date). **s.16(5)** (inserted retrospectively from 1 July 2017 by the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024): for FY 2017-18 to 2020-21, ITC claimed in any GSTR-3B filed up to 30 November 2021 is within time; **s.16(6)**: ITC after revocation of a cancelled registration. Demands founded on s.16(4) for those years should be closed under Circular 237/31/2024 (verify number) — plead it.
- s.17(5): blocked credits (motor vehicles, food and beverages, club membership, works contract/construction for immovable property — note the Supreme Court's *Safari Retreats* reading of "plant or machinery" (2024) and the 2024 retrospective amendment substituting "plant and machinery" — verify the current text), goods lost/stolen/destroyed/gifted, personal consumption, composition supplies, CSR (from 1 Oct 2023).
- Supplier non-payment (s.16(2)(c)) and non-existent/cancelled suppliers: the taxpayer's case is Circular 183/15/2022 and 193/05/2023 (2A vs 3B mismatch procedure for FY 2017-18 to 2021-22 — certificate from the supplier/CA), Press Release of 4 May 2018 (no automatic reversal for supplier default), *Suncraft Energy* (Calcutta HC, 2023; SLP dismissed) — recovery first from the defaulting supplier; contrary view in some High Courts. Reconcile 2A/2B with 3B and purchase register, and show payment to the supplier.
- Rule 36(4) (provisional ITC cap, 20% → 10% → 5% → nil from 1 Jan 2022) and Rule 37 (180-day reversal) are period-specific; check which version applied.

## 5. Jurisdiction and procedure objections

- **Proper officer** (s.2(91)): monetary limits for Superintendent / Deputy–Assistant Commissioner / Additional–Joint Commissioner under Circular 31/05/2018 (verify the current circular); an SCN by an officer above or below the limit is challengeable.
- **Cross-empowerment** (s.6): a taxpayer assigned to the State administration cannot be proceeded against by the Centre for the same subject matter and vice versa (s.6(2)(b)); parallel proceedings on the same period/subject are barred — plead the assignment letter and any pending State/Central proceeding.
- **DGGI/intelligence** notices: all-India jurisdiction under the 2017 notification but subject to s.6(2)(b).
- **Service** (s.169): hand delivery, registered post, email, portal upload, or publication — portal upload under "additional notices" has been held valid service by some High Courts and insufficient by others when the taxpayer was never alerted; check the dashboard history.
- **Consolidated multi-year SCNs** have been struck down by some High Courts (period-wise limitation) — plead where it helps.

## 6. Rates ("GST 2.0") — only where classification is in issue

From 22 September 2025 (56th GST Council, Sept 2025): slabs rationalised to 5% (merit) and 18% (standard), a 40% de-merit rate on specified goods and services, nil on individual life/health insurance; most 28% items moved to 18% and most 12% items to 5%. A classification dispute spanning the change must be split at the date; the rate notification (1/2017-CT (Rate) as amended) is the source.

## 7. Decisions to know (verify each on the court's portal)

- *Safari Retreats Pvt Ltd v. Chief Commissioner of CGST*, 2024 INSC 756 — "plant or machinery" in s.17(5)(d) read functionally; legislature then amended retrospectively.
- *Suncraft Energy Pvt Ltd v. Assistant Commissioner* (Calcutta HC, 2023; SLP dismissed 2023) — no reversal of ITC for supplier default without first proceeding against the supplier, absent collusion.
- *Mohit Minerals v. Union of India*, 2022 INSC 585 — GST Council recommendations are not binding on the legislature; ocean-freight RCM struck down.
- *Radha Krishan Industries v. State of H.P.*, 2021 INSC 265 — provisional attachment under s.83 is draconian; conditions must be strictly met.
- *Bharti Airtel v. Union of India* (SC, 2021) — GSTR-3B is a return; rectification governed by the statute, not equity.
- *Mahadeo Construction* (Jharkhand HC, 2020) and later cases — interest and recovery need an adjudication; summary recovery without SCN barred.
- *Shree Lalit Trading / Chennai* line (various HCs) — orders without personal hearing set aside under s.75(4).
- Supreme Court on s.168A extensions (Not. 56/2023) — track the transferred petitions; High Courts split (Gauhati HC struck down; others upheld) — verify current status.
