# DPDP obligations checklist (audit order)

Work top to bottom; a "no" early on changes everything below it. Section numbers refer to the DPDP Act, 2023; rule numbers refer to the DPDP Rules, 2025 as notified in November 2025 — **verify rule numbers and commencement on e-Gazette before citing them**, and record the date checked. Mark each item: Met / Partly / Not met / N/A, with the evidence seen.

## 1. Applicability (s.3)

- [ ] The data is personal data about identifiable individuals (s.2(t)) and is digital — collected in digital form or digitised afterwards (s.3(a)).
- [ ] Processing happens in India, **or** outside India in connection with offering goods or services to Data Principals in India (s.3(b)).
- [ ] No exclusion: not personal/domestic use; not data the Data Principal made publicly available themselves, or that was made public under a legal obligation (s.3(c)).
- [ ] No s.17 exemption claimed without a basis: legal claims and rights enforcement, courts and tribunals, prevention/investigation of offences, foreign contracts processed in India, mergers approved by a court, debt-default assessment; State instrumentalities, research/archiving/statistics, and startups only **by notification**.

## 2. Role (ss.2(i), 2(k), 8(2), 10)

- [ ] For each activity: Data Fiduciary (decides purpose and means) or Data Processor (on the Fiduciary's instructions under a valid contract).
- [ ] Processor engagements are under a written contract (s.8(2)); the Fiduciary remains responsible for compliance regardless.
- [ ] SDF status: has the Government notified the entity under s.10? If not, assess likelihood on the s.10 factors (volume and sensitivity, risk to rights, sovereignty and integrity, electoral democracy, security, public order).

## 3. Lawful ground per activity (ss.4, 6, 7)

- [ ] Every activity has a ground: consent (s.6) or a listed legitimate use (s.7). No "legitimate interest" or "contractual necessity" ground exists.
- [ ] Consent-based activities: purpose is specific and limited; consent is free, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, by clear affirmative action (s.6(1)); no bundling with unrelated purposes or with terms of service; no pre-ticked boxes or dark patterns.
- [ ] s.7 activities are genuinely within the listed uses (voluntary provision for a specified purpose; State benefits and functions; legal obligation; court order; medical emergency; epidemic/public health; disaster; **employment purposes** and safeguarding the employer).
- [ ] Marketing, profiling, analytics and sharing with third parties for their purposes rest on consent unless clearly within s.7.
- [ ] Consent obtained **before s.5 of the DPDP Act commenced** (the 18-month phase — around May 2027, verify): a notice must be given "as soon as reasonably practicable" to Data Principals whose consent was obtained earlier (s.5(2)); processing may continue until withdrawal.

## 4. Notice (s.5; Rule on notice)

- [ ] Given **before or at** the time of seeking consent, and for pre-existing consents as soon as practicable.
- [ ] Contains: itemised description of the personal data; the specified purposes; the manner of exercising rights (withdrawal, grievance, erasure); the manner of making a complaint to the Data Protection Board.
- [ ] Standalone and understandable independently of other information (the Rules require it to be presented independently of any other content).
- [ ] Available in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule, at the Data Principal's option (s.5(3)).
- [ ] Includes the business contact of the DPO (SDF) or the person able to answer questions about processing (s.8(9)).

## 5. Consent mechanics (s.6)

- [ ] Withdrawal is as easy as giving consent (s.6(4)); withdrawal stops processing within a reasonable time and does not affect prior lawful processing (s.6(5)–(6)).
- [ ] Consent records are kept and retrievable per Data Principal (the Rules require Consent Managers to keep records; Fiduciaries should be able to prove consent in a Board inquiry — s.6(10) places the burden on the Fiduciary).
- [ ] Consent Manager interface supported where used (s.6(7)–(9)); Consent Managers must be registered with the Board (Rule 4 — in force 12 months after notification, November 2026 — verify), be Indian companies meeting the First Schedule conditions, act as fiduciaries to Data Principals, and provide an interoperable platform.
- [ ] Persons with disabilities: consent through a lawful guardian where applicable (s.9(1)).

## 6. Children (s.9; Rules on verifiable consent and exemptions)

- [ ] Age is established well enough to know whether the user is under 18.
- [ ] **Verifiable** consent of a parent or lawful guardian obtained before processing a child's data; the Rules contemplate verifying the parent's identity and age through reliable details already held, a Government-issued identity, or a virtual token (e.g. DigiLocker-type) — verify the notified route.
- [ ] No processing likely to cause a detrimental effect on the child's well-being (s.9(2)).
- [ ] No tracking, behavioural monitoring, or targeted advertising directed at children (s.9(3)).
- [ ] Any reliance on an exemption is documented and matches the notified text (verify on e-Gazette): the Rules' **Fourth Schedule** exempts, in **Part A**, specified classes of Data Fiduciaries — clinical establishments, mental-health establishments and healthcare professionals (for health services), allied healthcare professionals, educational institutions (for educational activities and the child's safety), and crèche/child-care providers (for the child's safety) — from the tracking/monitoring/targeted-advertising bar and from verifiable parental consent for those purposes only; and, in **Part B**, specified purposes for any Fiduciary — creating a user account for email, blocking content detrimental to the child, and processing solely to confirm that the user is not a child. A Government notification under s.9(4)–(5) can add others.

## 7. Security safeguards (s.8(5); Rule on security)

- [ ] Reasonable security safeguards to prevent breach, including by processors.
- [ ] Minimum measures in the Rules: encryption, obfuscation, masking or tokens; access control; monitoring, logging and review to detect unauthorised access; logs and related data retained for one year (unless a law requires longer); data backups for continuity; contractual security obligations on processors.
- [ ] These are also the CERT-In / sectoral baseline — check ISO 27001 / sector circulars; DPDP does not reduce them.

## 8. Breach notification (s.8(6); Rule on intimation)

- [ ] A breach process exists that covers **any** personal data breach (unauthorised processing, accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction, or loss of access).
- [ ] To **each affected Data Principal**, without delay, in plain language: nature, extent, timing and location; likely consequences; mitigation taken; safety measures the individual may take; contact person.
- [ ] To the **Board**: an initial intimation without delay, then a detailed report within **72 hours** (or a longer period the Board allows) with facts, circumstances, reasons, mitigation, findings about the person who caused it, remedial measures, and the report of intimations to Data Principals (verify against the notified rule).
- [ ] Parallel clocks: CERT-In directions (6 hours to report specified incidents; 180-day logs), RBI (banks/NBFCs/PSOs — 2–6 hours), SEBI/IRDAI circulars, contractual notices to customers. Map them side by side.

## 9. Retention and erasure (s.8(7)–(8); Rule and Third Schedule)

- [ ] Personal data is erased (and processors made to erase) when the Data Principal withdraws consent **or** as soon as it is reasonable to assume the specified purpose is no longer served, whichever is earlier, unless retention is required by law.
- [ ] Retention periods are defined per activity and justified (tax, KYC, employment, litigation hold).
- [ ] If the entity is in the Rules' **Third Schedule** classes — e-commerce entity with ≥ 2 crore registered users in India, online gaming intermediary with ≥ 50 lakh, social-media intermediary with ≥ 2 crore (verify the notified thresholds) — personal data must be erased after **three years** from the later of the Data Principal's last approach for the specified purpose or the Rules' commencement, unless retention is required by law, with a notice at least **48 hours** before erasure telling the Data Principal they can log in or otherwise approach to keep it.
- [ ] Logs and related personal data are kept for one year after erasure of the underlying data where the Rules require it.

## 10. Accuracy and purpose limitation (s.8(3)–(4))

- [ ] Completeness, accuracy and consistency are ensured where the data is used to make a decision affecting the Data Principal or is disclosed to another Fiduciary.
- [ ] Processing is limited to the purpose specified in the notice; a new purpose needs a new ground.

## 11. Data Principal rights (ss.11–14; Rule on rights)

- [ ] **Access** (s.11): summary of personal data processed, identities of Fiduciaries and Processors it was shared with, and other prescribed information — with the carve-out for sharing with law-enforcement-type fiduciaries.
- [ ] **Correction, completion, updating, erasure** (s.12): a process exists; erasure requests are honoured unless retention is required by law.
- [ ] **Grievance redressal** (s.13): a readily available means; a published response period (the Rules cap it — 90 days in the notified text — verify) that the Fiduciary and any Consent Manager must meet; the Data Principal must exhaust this before going to the Board.
- [ ] **Nomination** (s.14): a way to nominate a person to exercise rights on death or incapacity.
- [ ] The manner of exercising each right is published, including the identifiers the Data Principal must use.
- [ ] Data Principal duties (s.15) — no impersonation, no suppression of material information, no false or frivolous grievances — are noted in the notice (useful, not mandatory).

## 12. Processors and contracts (s.8(2))

- [ ] Written contract with every processor covering: purpose limitation, security measures, sub-processing, breach assistance and notification timelines, erasure or return on termination, audit and cooperation.
- [ ] Processors are not making independent purpose decisions (if they are, they are Fiduciaries).

## 13. Cross-border transfers (s.16)

- [ ] No transfers to a country or territory on the Government's **restricted list** (notified under s.16(1); none may be in force — verify).
- [ ] Stricter sectoral localisation respected: RBI payment-system data (April 2018 directions — storage only in India, with permitted processing abroad and 24-hour repatriation), RBI/SEBI/IRDAI outsourcing and cloud circulars, telecom licence conditions. s.16(2) preserves these.
- [ ] SDF-specific restrictions on transferring specified categories outside India (Rules) checked if notified as an SDF.

## 14. SDF extras (s.10; Rule on SDFs)

- [ ] DPO appointed, based in India, reporting to the board/governing body, named as the grievance contact.
- [ ] Independent data auditor appointed; periodic audit conducted.
- [ ] DPIA conducted at the prescribed interval (the Rules say annually — verify) and the audit/DPIA reports' significant observations furnished to the Board.
- [ ] Algorithmic software due diligence done so that algorithms do not risk Data Principal rights.
- [ ] Any Government-specified categories of personal data and traffic data are not transferred outside India.

## 15. Governance and records

- [ ] A processing inventory (record of activities) exists and is current — not mandated by the Act in terms, but required in practice to answer s.11 access requests and Board inquiries.
- [ ] Published contact details of the DPO or responsible person (s.8(9)) on the website/app and in the notice.
- [ ] Board readiness: the Board works digitally (ss.18–28); inquiries follow the Rules' procedure; appeals to the TDSAT within 60 days (s.29; the Rules set the fee and digital filing).
- [ ] Training and an internal escalation path for breaches, rights requests and Board correspondence.
- [ ] A dated compliance calendar built around the phased commencement: immediate (Board, definitions), November 2026 (Consent Manager registration), May 2027 (Fiduciary obligations) — "as notified; verify".
