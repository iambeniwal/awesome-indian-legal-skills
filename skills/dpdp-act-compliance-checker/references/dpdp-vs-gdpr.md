# DPDP Act vs GDPR — the deltas that matter

For organisations already aligned with the GDPR. "Stricter" / "looser" is from the organisation's point of view. Verify section and rule references on India Code / e-Gazette before citing.

| Topic | GDPR | DPDP Act, 2023 + Rules, 2025 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scope of data | Personal data in any form, including manual filing systems | **Digital** personal data only (digitally collected or later digitised) — s.3 | Looser |
| Territorial reach | Establishment in EU; offering goods/services or monitoring behaviour of EU data subjects | Processing in India; processing abroad in connection with offering goods/services to Data Principals in India — s.3(b). No "monitoring" limb | Narrower |
| Special categories | Art. 9 special-category data with extra conditions | **No special-category concept**; sectoral rules (RBI, IRDAI, health) add their own | Looser on paper; stricter sectorally |
| Lawful bases | Six, including contract, legitimate interests, vital interests, public task | **Two**: consent (s.6) and "certain legitimate uses" (s.7). **No legitimate-interest or contractual-necessity ground**; marketing, analytics and most third-party sharing need consent | Stricter |
| Consent standard | Freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous | Same adjectives plus **unconditional** and "clear affirmative action"; withdrawal as easy as giving; burden of proof on the Fiduciary (s.6(10)) | Comparable, slightly stricter |
| Notice | Arts. 13–14 — long list of items | s.5 — shorter list (data itemised, purposes, rights, Board complaint route), but must be **standalone** and available in any **Eighth-Schedule language** on request | Different; language duty is stricter |
| Consent Managers | No equivalent | Registered Indian companies offering an interoperable consent platform; act as fiduciaries to the Data Principal — s.6(7)–(9), Rule 4 | New construct |
| Children | Under 16 (member states may lower to 13) for information-society services; parental consent for those | **Under 18**, all contexts; **verifiable** parental consent; **ban** on tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads directed at children — s.9 | Stricter |
| Employee data | Needs a basis; consent disfavoured | "Employment purposes" is a listed legitimate use (s.7) — no consent needed for ordinary HR processing | Looser |
| DPIA | Required for high-risk processing (Art. 35) for any controller | Only **SDFs** must conduct periodic DPIAs — s.10 | Looser for non-SDFs |
| DPO | Required for public bodies and large-scale/sensitive processing | Only **SDFs**; DPO must be in India and report to the board — s.10. Others publish a contact person — s.8(9) | Looser for non-SDFs |
| Records of processing | Art. 30 | Not expressly mandated for all; needed in practice for s.11 access and Board inquiries | Looser on paper |
| Breach notification | To authority within 72 hours if risk; to individuals only if high risk | To the Board **and every affected Data Principal** for **any** breach, no risk threshold; initial intimation without delay, detailed report to the Board within 72 hours — s.8(6), Rules | Stricter |
| Retention | Storage limitation principle | Erase when purpose served or consent withdrawn; Rules add fixed inactivity-based erasure for large e-commerce/gaming/social-media platforms with 48-hour notice; one-year log retention | Comparable; more prescriptive for large platforms |
| Data subject rights | Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, automated decision-making | Access (summary), correction/erasure, grievance redressal, **nomination**. **No portability, restriction, objection or Art. 22 right** | Narrower |
| Grievance | Complaint to DPA directly | Must exhaust the Fiduciary's grievance mechanism first (s.13); published response period capped by the Rules | Different |
| Data subject duties | None | s.15 duties: no impersonation, no suppression of material information, no false grievances; penalty up to ₹10,000 | New |
| Cross-border | Adequacy, SCCs, BCRs; transfer impact assessments | Transfers allowed **except** to Government-notified restricted countries (negative list) — s.16; sectoral localisation (RBI payments) prevails; SDFs may face category restrictions | Looser generally; stricter sectorally |
| Processors | Art. 28 contract with mandatory clauses; direct processor obligations | Processor acts under contract; **Fiduciary bears responsibility**; no direct statutory duties on processors (s.8(2)) | Looser for processors, stricter for fiduciaries |
| Regulator | Independent DPAs; one-stop shop | Data Protection Board of India — adjudicatory, digital-first; Government holds rule-making and notification powers (SDF, exemptions, restricted countries) | Different |
| Penalties | Up to €20m / 4% global turnover | Fixed caps per breach type in the Schedule: up to ₹250 crore (security safeguards), ₹200 crore (breach notification; children), ₹150 crore (SDF duties), ₹50 crore (other), ₹10,000 (Data Principal duties) — s.33 factors govern quantum (verify) | Different structure |
| Appeals | National courts | TDSAT within 60 days — s.29 | Different |
| Exemptions | Limited (Art. 23 by law) | Broad s.17: State instrumentalities by notification, research/archiving/statistics, startups by notification, legal claims, investigation, M&A approved by court | Looser |
| Government access | Member-state law, subject to EU principles | s.17(2) exemption for notified State instrumentalities; s.36 power to call for information | Looser for the State |

## Practical reading

- GDPR records, DPIAs, processor contracts and security controls **carry over** and shorten the DPDP gap list.
- GDPR **legal-basis mapping does not**: every activity resting on legitimate interests or contractual necessity must be re-grounded on consent or a s.7 use.
- The **children's regime** usually produces the largest delta: age thresholds, verification, and the advertising ban.
- The **breach regime** produces the second: all breaches, all affected individuals, two Board filings, plus CERT-In's 6-hour clock.
- Keep GDPR vocabulary out of Indian notices: "controller", "legitimate interest", "data subject" have no DPDP meaning; use Data Fiduciary, Data Principal, consent, legitimate use.
