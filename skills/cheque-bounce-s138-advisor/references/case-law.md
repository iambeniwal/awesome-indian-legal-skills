# s.138 NI Act — controlling decisions by issue

Citations are given in the reporter form most practitioners use. Confirm each on [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/) (search by title; newer judgments carry a neutral citation `YYYY INSC NNN`) before putting it in a filing. Holdings below are one-line summaries, not quotations.

## Presumption and burden

- **Rangappa v. Sri Mohan**, (2010) 11 SCC 441 — the s.139 presumption extends to the existence of a legally enforceable debt; the accused rebuts it on a preponderance of probabilities, including from the complainant's own material.
- **Bir Singh v. Mukesh Kumar**, (2019) 4 SCC 197 — a signed blank cheque voluntarily handed over attracts s.139 even if the details were filled in by someone else.
- **Basalingappa v. Mudibasappa**, (2019) 5 SCC 418 — the accused need not lead evidence to rebut; the complainant's financial capacity can be put in issue.
- **Kalamani Tex v. P. Balasubramanian**, (2021) 5 SCC 283 — once signature and issuance are admitted, the presumptions under s.118(a) and s.139 arise together.

## Which dishonours count

- **NEPC Micon Ltd v. Magma Leasing Ltd**, (1999) 4 SCC 253 — "account closed" is within s.138.
- **Modi Cements Ltd v. Kuchil Kumar Nandi**, (1998) 3 SCC 249 — "stop payment" instructions do not take the case out of s.138.
- **Laxmi Dyechem v. State of Gujarat**, (2012) 13 SCC 375 — "signature mismatch"/"image not matching" can attract s.138.

## Notice

- **K. Bhaskaran v. Sankaran Vaidhyan Balan**, (1999) 7 SCC 510 — the five components of the offence; the notice's purpose is to give the drawer a chance to pay; 15 days run from receipt.
- **Suman Sethi v. Ajay K. Churiwal**, (2000) 2 SCC 380 — a notice demanding the cheque amount plus separable interest/costs is valid; an omnibus demand that does not identify the cheque amount is not.
- **Central Bank of India v. Saxons Farms**, (1999) 8 SCC 221 — no particular form of notice; substance matters.
- **MSR Leathers v. S. Palaniappan**, (2013) 1 SCC 177 — a cheque may be presented more than once within validity; a notice after any dishonour gives a fresh cause of action if the earlier one was not sued on.

## Service

- **C.C. Alavi Haji v. Palapetty Muhammed**, (2007) 6 SCC 555 — notice sent by registered post to the correct address is deemed served under s.27 General Clauses Act and s.114 Evidence Act (now s.119 BSA); a drawer claiming non-receipt can pay within 15 days of receiving the summons.
- **Ajeet Seeds Ltd v. K. Gopala Krishnaiah**, (2014) 12 SCC 685 — the complaint need not plead the AD card; a presumption of service arises from correct addressing and dispatch.

## Limitation and premature complaints

- **Yogendra Pratap Singh v. Savitri Pandey**, (2014) 10 SCC 713 — a complaint filed before the 15-day period expires is not maintainable and cannot be revived; the complainant may file afresh if within limitation.
- **Econ Antri Ltd v. Rom Industries Ltd**, (2014) 11 SCC 769 — in computing one month under s.142(b), exclude the day on which the cause of action arose.
- **Saketh India Ltd v. India Securities Ltd**, (1999) 3 SCC 1 — same exclusion principle under s.12 Limitation Act / s.9 General Clauses Act.
- **Pawan Kumar Ralli v. Maninder Singh Narula**, (2014) 15 SCC 245 — condonation under the proviso to s.142(b) may be sought even at a later stage if sufficient cause is shown.

## Territorial jurisdiction

- **Dashrath Rupsingh Rathod v. State of Maharashtra**, (2014) 9 SCC 129 — *superseded* by the 2015 amendment inserting s.142(2) and s.142A; cite only for history.
- **Bridgestone India Pvt Ltd v. Inderpal Singh**, (2016) 2 SCC 75 — the 2015 amendment applies to pending cases; jurisdiction lies where the payee's bank branch is.
- **Himalaya Self Farming Group v. Goyal Feed Suppliers**, 2020 SCC OnLine SC 889 — forum follows s.142(2); the complainant's bank branch controls when presented through an account.

## Company and partner liability (s.141)

- **S.M.S. Pharmaceuticals Ltd v. Neeta Bhalla**, (2005) 8 SCC 89 — the complaint must specifically aver that the accused was in charge of and responsible for the conduct of the company's business; the managing director/joint MD is covered by role; the signatory is covered by s.141(2).
- **National Small Industries Corp v. Harmeet Singh Paintal**, (2010) 3 SCC 330 — vicarious liability requires specific averments; a bald reproduction of s.141 is insufficient for non-signatory directors.
- **Aneeta Hada v. Godfather Travels & Tours**, (2012) 5 SCC 661 — the company must be arraigned for directors to be prosecuted under s.141 (except where the company cannot be, e.g. dissolved).
- **Sunita Palita v. Panchami Stone Quarry**, (2022) 10 SCC 152 — independent/non-executive directors without specific role allegations should not be summoned.
- **Ashok Shewakramani v. State of A.P.**, 2023 INSC 692 — "in charge of" and "responsible for the conduct of business" are both required; mere directorship is not enough.

## Compounding, settlement, interim compensation

- **Damodar S. Prabhu v. Sayed Babalal H.**, (2010) 5 SCC 663 — compounding under s.147 is permitted at any stage; graded costs (nil at first hearing, rising to 10%, 15%, 20% of the cheque amount at later stages) paid to legal services authorities.
- **Meters and Instruments Pvt Ltd v. Kanchan Mehta**, (2018) 1 SCC 560 — s.138 is primarily compensatory; courts may close proceedings on full payment even without the complainant's consent (read with *Expeditious Trial* below).
- **G.J. Raja v. Tejraj Surana**, (2019) 19 SCC 469 — s.143A (interim compensation up to 20%) is prospective, applying to complaints filed after 1 September 2018.
- **Surinder Singh Deswal v. Virender Gandhi**, (2019) 11 SCC 341 — s.148 deposit on appeal applies to appeals filed after the amendment even for earlier complaints.
- **Rakesh Ranjan Shrivastava v. State of Jharkhand**, 2024 INSC 205 — interim compensation under s.143A is discretionary, not automatic; the court must record reasons and consider a prima facie case.

## Trial management

- **Expeditious Trial of Cases under Section 138 of NI Act, 1881, In re**, (2021) 16 SCC 116 (suo motu) — guidelines on summary trial, service via email/dasti, no fresh evidence on conversion to summons trial, single trial for multiple cheques within 12 months, and the limited scope of inherent-power quashing at the summons stage.
- **Indian Bank Association v. Union of India**, (2014) 5 SCC 590 — directions for swift disposal: evidence by affidavit, service on the same day, summons returnable within limited time.

## Debt, security cheques and defences

- **Sampelly Satyanarayana Rao v. Indian Renewable Energy Development Agency**, (2016) 10 SCC 458 — a post-dated cheque for an instalment due is for a legally enforceable debt; a "security" label does not help once the liability has crystallised.
- **Sunil Todi v. State of Gujarat**, 2021 SCC OnLine SC 1174 — "security cheque" is not a magic phrase; the test is whether a debt or liability subsisted when the cheque was presented.
- **Dashrathbhai Trikambhai Patel v. Hitesh Mahendrabhai Patel**, 2022 INSC 1080 — part-payment before presentment must be endorsed on the cheque (s.56 NI Act); otherwise the cheque for the full amount is not for the amount then due and s.138 is not attracted.
- **Dalmia Cement (Bharat) Ltd v. Galaxy Traders**, (2001) 6 SCC 463 — s.138 is a regulatory offence aimed at ensuring credibility of cheques; interpret to advance the object.
