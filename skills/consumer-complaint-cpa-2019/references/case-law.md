# Consumer Protection Act — controlling decisions by issue

Most are under the 1986 Act; the 2019 Act carries the same definitions forward with additions, and the Supreme Court has applied the older rulings to it. Confirm each on [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/) (neutral citation `YYYY INSC NNN` for recent ones) or [ncdrc.nic.in](https://ncdrc.nic.in/) before filing. Holdings are one-line summaries, not quotations.

## Who is a consumer; commercial purpose

- **Laxmi Engineering Works v. P.S.G. Industrial Institute**, (1995) 3 SCC 583 — "commercial purpose" is a question of fact; goods bought to earn a livelihood by self-employment are not for a commercial purpose.
- **Lilavati Kirtilal Mehta Medical Trust v. Unique Shanti Developers**, (2020) 2 SCC 265 — the test is the dominant purpose and a close and direct nexus with profit-generation; a trust buying flats to house nurses was a consumer.
- **National Insurance Co. Ltd v. Harsolia Motors**, 2023 INSC 325 — an insurance policy taken by a commercial enterprise to indemnify loss is not a service hired for a commercial purpose; the insured is a consumer.
- **Shrikant G. Mantri v. Punjab National Bank**, 2022 INSC 215 — an overdraft facility availed to expand business is for a commercial purpose; the borrower is not a consumer.
- **Rohit Chaudhary v. Vipul Ltd**, 2023 INSC 807 — the livelihood/self-employment exception applied to a commercial unit bought for the buyer's own business use.
- **Bar of Indian Lawyers v. D.K. Gandhi PS National Institute of Communicable Diseases**, 2024 INSC 410 — services of advocates are not "service" under the Act; professionals rendering services requiring high skill are distinguished from business/trade. *Note: the Court doubted V.P. Shantha's extension to professions and referred the question of medical professionals to a larger bench — check status.*

## Services covered

- **Indian Medical Association v. V.P. Shantha**, (1995) 6 SCC 651 — medical services are "service"; services rendered free of charge to everyone are not; a hospital charging some patients is covered for all.
- **Lucknow Development Authority v. M.K. Gupta**, (1994) 1 SCC 243 — statutory authorities providing housing are service providers; compensation for harassment and mental agony is available; the authority may recover from the erring officer.
- **Maharshi Dayanand University v. Surjeet Kaur**, (2010) 11 SCC 159 — a university conducting examinations and conferring degrees is not rendering "service"; the NCDRC in **Manu Solanki v. Vinayaka Mission University** (2020) applied this to educational institutions broadly, and the point remains contested — plead with caution.
- **Ghaziabad Development Authority v. Balbir Singh**, (2004) 5 SCC 65 — compensation must be reasoned and tied to loss; guidance on interest and compensation for delayed allotment.

## Pecuniary jurisdiction

- **Pyaridevi Chabiraj Steels Pvt Ltd v. National Insurance Co. Ltd**, NCDRC, Consumer Case No. 833 of 2020 (28 August 2020) — under the 2019 Act pecuniary jurisdiction is determined by the value of goods or services **paid as consideration**, not by the compensation claimed.
- **Renu Singh v. Experion Developers Pvt Ltd**, NCDRC 2022 — on what counts as consideration "paid" for a flat booked on instalments; check the latest NCDRC and State Commission practice.
- Consumer Protection (Jurisdiction of the District Commission, the State Commission and the National Commission) Rules, 2021 (w.e.f. 30 December 2021) — revised the limits to ₹50 lakh / ₹2 crore.

## Builders, RERA and arbitration

- **Experion Developers Pvt Ltd v. Sushma Ashok Shiroor**, 2022 INSC 374 — a homebuyer may approach the consumer forum despite RERA; refund with interest for delayed possession; compensation is for the buyer's loss, not a penalty.
- **Imperia Structures Ltd v. Anil Patni**, (2020) 10 SCC 783 — RERA does not bar consumer complaints; s.100 CPA and s.88 RERA are both non-derogation clauses.
- **IREO Grace Realtech Pvt Ltd v. Abhishek Khanna**, 2021 INSC 22, (2021) 3 SCC 241 — one-sided clauses in a builder-buyer agreement are unfair; buyers are not bound by them; refund with 9% interest where possession was delayed beyond a reasonable period.
- **Emaar MGF Land Ltd v. Aftab Singh**, (2019) 12 SCC 751 — an arbitration clause does not oust the consumer commission's jurisdiction; the consumer may elect the forum.
- **Ambrish Kumar Shukla v. Ferrous Infrastructure Pvt Ltd**, NCDRC larger bench, CC 97 of 2016 (7 October 2016) — class complaints by flat-buyers with the same interest; pecuniary jurisdiction then aggregates the value of all flats; Order I Rule 8 procedure applies.
- **M/s Newtech Promoters and Developers v. State of U.P.**, 2021 INSC 718 — RERA's refund/interest jurisdiction and pre-deposit on appeal; useful when advising which route is faster.

## Insurance, banking, airlines, e-commerce

- **Canara Bank v. United India Insurance**, (2020) 3 SCC 455 — beneficiaries of an insurance policy taken for their benefit can be consumers.
- **Manjeet Singh v. National Insurance Co.**, (2018) 2 SCC 108 — repudiation on a technical ground without prejudice to the insurer is deficiency.
- **Interglobe Aviation Ltd v. N. Satchidanand**, (2011) 7 SCC 463 — an exclusive-jurisdiction clause in a ticket does not bind a consumer complaint; airlines are service providers.
- Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 — marketplace and inventory e-commerce entities' duties (grievance officer, country of origin, no manipulation of prices, returns/refund terms); plead the specific rule breached alongside s.2(47).

## Limitation and condonation

- **State Bank of India v. B.S. Agricultural Industries**, (2009) 5 SCC 121 — limitation under the Act is mandatory; a Commission must address it even if not raised; a time-barred complaint without condonation is a nullity.
- **Anshul Aggarwal v. New Okhla Industrial Development Authority**, (2011) 14 SCC 578 — the short limitation for appeals reflects the summary nature of the forum; delay must be explained day by day.

## Compensation and costs

- **Charan Singh v. Healing Touch Hospital**, (2000) 7 SCC 668 — compensation must be just, reasoned and related to the loss; Commissions should not be either niggardly or extravagant.
- **DLF Homes Panchkula Pvt Ltd v. D.S. Dhanda**, (2020) 16 SCC 318 — multiple heads of compensation for the same delay should not be awarded cumulatively without reason; a single reasonable rate of interest usually suffices.
