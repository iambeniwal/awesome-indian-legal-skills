# Limitation — controlling decisions by issue

Confirm each on [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/) before putting it in a filing; newer judgments carry a neutral citation `YYYY INSC NNN`. Holdings are one-line summaries, not quotations.

## Mandatory bar and the correct Article

- **Popat and Kotecha Property v. State Bank of India Staff Assn.**, (2005) 7 SCC 510 — s.3 is mandatory; a court must dismiss a time-barred suit even if limitation is not pleaded.
- **Khatri Hotels Pvt Ltd v. Union of India**, (2011) 9 SCC 126 — under Article 58 the period runs from when the right to sue *first* accrues; successive violations do not restart it.
- **Rajender Singh v. Santa Singh**, (1973) 2 SCC 705 — the Article is chosen by the substance of the relief, read with the facts, not the plaintiff's label.
- **Kerala State Electricity Board v. T.P. Kunhaliumma**, (1976) 4 SCC 634 — Article 137 covers applications to civil courts under any statute where no period is provided.
- **Prem Singh v. Birbal**, (2006) 5 SCC 353 — a suit to set aside a voidable instrument is under Article 59; a void document need not be set aside and Article 65/113 may govern.
- **Ahmadsahab Abdul Mulla v. Bibijan**, (2009) 5 SCC 462 — Article 54: "date fixed for performance" must be a date, not an event; otherwise the second limb (notice of refusal) applies.

## Computation (ss.4, 9, 12)

- **Econ Antri Ltd v. Rom Industries Ltd**, (2014) 11 SCC 769 and **Saketh India Ltd v. India Securities Ltd**, (1999) 3 SCC 1 — exclude the day from which the period is to be reckoned (s.12(1) / s.9 General Clauses Act).
- **State of M.P. v. Bherulal**, (2020) 10 SCC 654 — "three months" in s.34(3) Arbitration Act means calendar months, not 90 days; delay beyond 120 days cannot be condoned.
- **V. Nagarajan v. SKS Ispat and Power Ltd**, (2022) 2 SCC 244 — IBC s.61 runs from pronouncement of the order, not from upload or receipt; s.12(2) copy time is excluded only if a certified copy was applied for with diligence.
- **Lachhman Das Arora v. Ganeshi Lal**, (1999) 8 SCC 532 — s.4 applies only where the prescribed period expires on a day the court is closed; it does not extend a period that expired earlier.

## Condonation (s.5) and special statutes (s.29(2))

- **Collector, Land Acquisition v. Mst. Katiji**, (1987) 2 SCC 107 — "sufficient cause" is to be construed liberally to advance substantial justice; no presumption of deliberate delay.
- **Basawaraj v. Special Land Acquisition Officer**, (2013) 14 SCC 81 — liberality does not mean condoning negligence; the explanation must cover the delay.
- **Postmaster General v. Living Media India Ltd**, (2012) 3 SCC 563 — government departments get no special latitude for bureaucratic delay; a day-by-day explanation is expected.
- **Pathapati Subba Reddy v. Special Deputy Collector (LA)**, 2024 INSC 286 — s.5 is discretionary; the court weighs the merits only to the extent needed; long unexplained delays are not condoned on sympathy.
- **Union of India v. Popular Construction Co.**, (2001) 8 SCC 470 — s.34(3) Arbitration Act's "but not thereafter" excludes s.5.
- **Simplex Infrastructure Ltd v. Union of India**, (2017) 14 SCC 225 — no condonation beyond 120 days under s.34(3); s.14 may still exclude time in a wrong forum.
- **Consolidated Engineering Enterprises v. Principal Secretary, Irrigation Dept.**, (2008) 7 SCC 169 — s.14 Limitation Act applies to s.34 applications; s.29(2) imports ss.4–24 unless expressly excluded.
- **Government of Maharashtra v. Borse Brothers Engineers and Contractors**, (2021) 6 SCC 460 — s.37 appeals: 60 days under the Commercial Courts Act, Article 116/117 otherwise; s.5 applies but condonation is the exception, for short delays with a bona fide explanation.
- **Kalpraj Dharamshi v. Kotak Investment Advisors Ltd**, (2021) 10 SCC 401 — IBC s.61: outer limit of 45 days; s.14 may be applied where proceedings were pursued in a wrong forum.
- **Singh Enterprises v. CCE**, (2008) 3 SCC 70 — where a statute fixes an outer limit for condonation, the appellate authority cannot go beyond it and s.5 is excluded by implication.
- **Baleshwar Dayal Jaiswal v. Bank of India**, (2015) 15 SCC 433 — s.5 applies to applications under s.17 SARFAESI Act.

## s.14, s.17, s.18/19

- **M.P. Steel Corporation v. CCE**, (2015) 7 SCC 58 — s.14 principles apply even where the Limitation Act does not directly apply; "due diligence" and "good faith" are tested on facts.
- **Laxmi Engineering Works v. P.S.G. Industrial Institute**, (1995) 3 SCC 583 — s.14 applied to exclude time spent before a consumer forum that lacked jurisdiction.
- **Pallav Sheth v. Custodian**, (2001) 7 SCC 549 — s.17: fraud or concealment postpones the start of limitation until discovery with due diligence.
- **Asset Reconstruction Company (India) Ltd v. Bishal Jaiswal**, (2021) 6 SCC 366 — s.18 acknowledgment applies to IBC s.7 applications; balance-sheet entries can be acknowledgments.
- **Sampuran Singh v. Niranjan Kaur**, (1999) 2 SCC 679 — an acknowledgment must be made before the period expires; a later acknowledgment does not revive a barred debt.
- **Dena Bank v. C. Shivakumar Reddy**, (2021) 10 SCC 330 — a fresh period runs from a signed acknowledgment, and from a decree/recovery certificate for IBC purposes.

## Consumer, property and continuing causes

- **Meerut Development Authority v. Mukesh Kumar Gupta**, (2012) 12 SCC 736 (and the line of flat-delay cases) — non-delivery of possession is a continuing cause of action for consumer complaints.
- **Anshul Aggarwal v. New Okhla Industrial Development Authority**, (2011) 14 SCC 578 — consumer fora must address limitation at the threshold; condonation requires reasons.
- **Ravinder Kaur Grewal v. Manjit Kaur**, (2019) 8 SCC 729 — adverse possession (Article 65) can be used as a sword by a plaintiff who has perfected title, not only as a shield.
- **Balkrishan v. Satyaprakash**, (2001) 2 SCC 498 — continuing wrong under s.22: the wrong, not merely its effects, must continue.

## COVID-19 orders

- **In re Cognizance for Extension of Limitation**, Suo Motu W.P.(C) 3/2020 — orders of 23 March 2020, 8 March 2021, 27 April 2021, 23 September 2021 and the final order of 10 January 2022, (2022) 3 SCC 117 — the period 15 March 2020 to 28 February 2022 is excluded for all limitation periods under general and special laws, including outer limits; 90 days from 1 March 2022 where limitation would have expired within the excluded period.
- **Sagufa Ahmed v. Upper Assam Plywood Products Pvt Ltd**, (2021) 2 SCC 317 — the extension orders enlarged the *limitation period*, not the period up to which delay could be condoned (a position later addressed by the direction that outer limits are also excluded — apply the 10 January 2022 order, not the earlier reasoning).
- **Prakash Corporates v. Dee Vee Projects Ltd**, (2022) 5 SCC 112 — the exclusion extends to the time for filing a written statement under Order VIII CPC in commercial suits.
