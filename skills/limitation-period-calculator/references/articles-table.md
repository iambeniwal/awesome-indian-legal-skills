# Limitation periods — the ones practitioners use most

A shortlist, not the Schedule. Read the Article's full text on [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/) before relying on it: the third column ("time from which period begins to run") is where cases are won and lost. "Condonation" below means s.5 Limitation Act or the statute's own power; s.5 never applies to suits.

## Part I — Suits (Limitation Act, 1963, Schedule)

| Article | Suit | Period | Runs from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | Price of goods sold and delivered, no fixed credit | 3 years | Date of delivery |
| 15 | Price of goods sold on fixed credit | 3 years | Expiry of the credit period |
| 18 | Price of work done where no time fixed for payment | 3 years | When the work is done |
| 19 | Money payable for money lent | 3 years | When the loan is made |
| 21 | Money lent under an agreement that it is payable on demand | 3 years | When the loan is made |
| 22 | Money deposited under an agreement that it is payable on demand | 3 years | When the demand is made |
| 54 | Specific performance of a contract | 3 years | Date fixed for performance; if none, when the plaintiff has notice that performance is refused |
| 55 | Compensation for breach of contract (not specially provided for) | 3 years | When the contract is broken; for a continuing breach, when it ceases |
| 56 | Declaration that a registered instrument is forged | 3 years | When the registration becomes known to the plaintiff |
| 58 | Any other declaration | 3 years | When the right to sue first accrues |
| 59 | To cancel or set aside an instrument or decree, or rescission | 3 years | When the facts entitling the plaintiff to that relief first become known |
| 61 | By a mortgagor to redeem or recover possession | 30 years | When the right to redeem accrues |
| 62 | To enforce payment of money secured by mortgage or charged on immovable property | 12 years | When the money becomes due |
| 64 | Possession of immovable property based on previous possession (not title), when dispossessed | 12 years | Date of dispossession |
| 65 | Possession of immovable property or an interest in it based on title | 12 years | When the defendant's possession becomes adverse to the plaintiff |
| 70 | To recover movable property conveyed in trust and afterwards transferred for value | 3 years | When the transfer becomes known |
| 72 | Compensation for an act done in pursuance of an enactment | 1 year | When the act or omission takes place |
| 74 | Malicious prosecution | 1 year | When the plaintiff is acquitted or the prosecution otherwise terminates |
| 75/76 | Libel / slander | 1 year | When the libel is published / when words are spoken (or special damage results) |
| 110 | By a person excluded from a joint family property to enforce a right to share | 12 years | When the exclusion becomes known |
| 111 | Pre-emption | 1 year | When the purchaser takes physical possession (or registration if registered) |
| 112 | Any suit by or on behalf of the Central or a State Government (except before the Supreme Court) | 30 years | As for a like suit by a private person |
| 113 | Residuary — any suit for which no period is provided elsewhere | 3 years | When the right to sue accrues |

Useful sub-rules: s.10 — no limitation for suits against express trustees for trust property; s.22 — continuing breach/tort gives a fresh period every moment but damages are confined to the period within limitation; Article 113 is the fallback only after every specific Article has been ruled out.

## Part II — Appeals (Limitation Act Schedule)

| Article | Appeal | Period | Runs from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 114 | Against an order of acquittal (a) to the High Court — 90 days; (b) other courts — 30 days; (c) by the complainant under special leave — 30 days from grant of leave | as stated | Date of the order of acquittal (or grant of leave) |
| 115 | Against a sentence or an order (not acquittal) (a) to the High Court — 60 days; (b) to any other court — 30 days | as stated | Date of the sentence or order |
| 116 | Under the CPC (a) to a High Court from any decree or order — 90 days; (b) to any other court — 30 days | as stated | Date of the decree or order |
| 117 | From a decree or order of any High Court to the same court (Letters Patent / intra-court) | 30 days | Date of the decree or order |

s.12(2): in computing an appeal period, exclude the day the judgment was pronounced and the time requisite for obtaining a copy of the decree/order (s.12(3): and of the judgment where a decree is appealed). s.5 applies. Second appeals under s.100 CPC fall under Article 116(a).

| Article | Application | Period | Runs from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 122 | To restore a suit or appeal or application dismissed for default or want of prosecution | 30 days | Date of dismissal |
| 123 | To set aside a decree passed ex parte (or to re-hear an appeal decreed ex parte) | 30 days | Date of the decree; where summons was not duly served, when the applicant had knowledge of the decree |
| 124 | For review of a judgment by a court other than the Supreme Court | 30 days | Date of the decree or order |
| 125 | To record an adjustment or satisfaction of a decree | 30 days | When the payment or adjustment is made |
| 126 | For leave to appear and defend a summary suit (Order XXXVII) | 10 days | When the summons is served |
| 127 | To set aside a sale in execution of a decree | 60 days | Date of the sale |
| 131 | For revision under the CPC or the CrPC/BNSS | 90 days | Date of the decree, sentence or order |
| 134 | For delivery of possession by a purchaser at an execution sale | 1 year | When the sale becomes absolute |
| 136 | For execution of any decree (other than a mandatory injunction) or order | 12 years | When the decree or order becomes enforceable (or, for payment at a future date, when default occurs) |

s.5 applies to applications other than those under Order XXI CPC (execution). Article 137 governs applications under special laws to civil courts where the special law is silent (*Kerala SEB v. T.P. Kunhaliumma*).

## Part V — The COVID-19 exclusion (apply with `--exclude-covid`)

*In re Cognizance for Extension of Limitation*, Suo Motu W.P.(C) 3/2020; final order of 10 January 2022:

1. The period **15 March 2020 to 28 February 2022** is excluded in computing limitation under any general or special law for all judicial and quasi-judicial proceedings.
2. The balance limitation remaining becomes available from 1 March 2022.
3. Where limitation would have expired between 15 March 2020 and 28 February 2022, the party has **90 days from 1 March 2022**, or the actual longer balance if that is more.
4. The excluded period also applies to outer limits for condonation and to periods under s.23(4) and s.29A Arbitration Act, s.12A Commercial Courts Act, and provisos (b) and (c) of s.138 NI Act.

The orders did not extend periods for events after 28 February 2022, and they do not help a cause that was already time-barred before 15 March 2020.
