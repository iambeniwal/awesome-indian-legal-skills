## 2. The Magistrate's limits under s.480 (437 CrPC)

A Magistrate shall not release a person where there appear reasonable grounds for believing the person is guilty of an offence punishable with **death or imprisonment for life**, nor where the offence is cognizable and the person was previously convicted of an offence punishable with death, life or seven years, or convicted twice or more of a cognizable offence punishable with three to seven years. Exceptions (first proviso): a person under sixteen, a woman, or a person who is sick or infirm may still be released. The second proviso lets the Magistrate release for any other special reason recorded. Check the current text — the BNSS reworded the provisos.

Also in s.480: if the trial of a Magistrate-triable offence is not concluded within sixty days from the first date fixed for evidence, the accused in custody is to be released unless the Magistrate records reasons otherwise (s.480(6), confirmed against the BNSS text).

## 3. Standard factors (regular and anticipatory bail)

Argue each with a fact and a document. Courts weigh them together; no single factor decides (*Prasanta Kumar Sarkar*, *Gudikanti Narasimhulu*).

| Factor | Points for the applicant | Points for the prosecution |
|---|---|---|
| Nature and gravity of the accusation | Offence punishable under seven years; no injury/recovery; private dispute | Death/life/minimum-sentence offence; organised or repeat conduct |
| Role attributed | General or omnibus allegation; no overt act; parity with co-accused on bail | Specific overt act; principal role; weapon or recovery from the applicant |
| Evidence and its stage | Investigation complete, charge sheet filed, nothing left to recover; evidence documentary and already seized | Investigation at a critical stage; custodial interrogation needed with recorded reasons |
| Flight risk | Roots in the community: family, employment, property, passport surrendered | Foreign residence, prior evasion, proclamation |
| Tampering / influencing witnesses | Witnesses are officials or strangers; statements recorded under s.183 (164 CrPC) | Threats on record; victim related or dependent; prior interference |
| Antecedents | Clean record (say so only if verified) | Prior cases, prior bail cancellations |
| Health, age, gender | Serious illness, age above sixty, pregnancy, sole carer | Medical care available in custody |
| Period undergone and delay in trial | Long incarceration with trial not in sight (*Manish Sisodia*, *K.A. Najeeb*) | Delay attributable to the accused |
| Arrest irregularity | No s.35(3) notice or reasons; grounds not furnished; delayed production | Compliance documented |
| Settlement / compounding | Compoundable offence, parties settled | Non-compoundable; victim opposes |

## 4. *Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI* (2022) categories

| Category | Offences | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Punishable with imprisonment up to seven years (IPC/BNS and other laws) | If the accused was not arrested during investigation and cooperated, the court takes cognizance and issues summons; on appearance, bail without custody. Interim bail while an application is pending |
| **B** | Punishable with death, life, or more than seven years | Bail on merits; the Category A relaxations do not apply automatically |
| **C** | Special Acts with their own bail restrictions — NDPS s.37, PMLA s.45, UAPA s.43D(5), Companies Act s.212(6) | The statutory test governs, read with Article 21 where incarceration is prolonged |
| **D** | Economic offences not covered by special Acts | Treated under Category A/B by punishment; gravity is a factor, not a bar (*Sanjay Chandra*, *P. Chidambaram*) |

Additional *Antil* directions worth citing: courts should not insist on custody at the charge-sheet stage for Category A accused who were never arrested; bail applications should ordinarily be decided within two weeks (anticipatory within six weeks); non-compliance with s.41/41A (now s.35) entitles the accused to bail; the State should consider a Bail Act.

## 5. Special statutes — do not use ordinary grounds alone

| Statute | Test | Note |
|---|---|---|
| NDPS Act s.37 | For commercial quantity and specified offences: the Public Prosecutor is heard, and the court is satisfied there are reasonable grounds to believe the accused is **not guilty** and is **not likely to commit any offence** while on bail | Small/intermediate quantity: ordinary test. Default bail: 180 days, extendable to one year (s.36A(4)). Long incarceration can tilt the balance (*Mohd. Muslim*, 2023) |
| UAPA s.43D(5) | Bail refused if, on the case diary/charge sheet, there are reasonable grounds for believing the accusation is prima facie true (*Watali*: no detailed weighing of evidence) | Article 21 can override after long incarceration with trial not in sight (*K.A. Najeeb*). Default bail: 90 days, extendable to 180 (s.43D(2)) |
| PMLA s.45 | Twin conditions: Public Prosecutor heard; reasonable grounds to believe the accused is not guilty and not likely to offend on bail | Upheld in *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary*; relaxed for women/sick/infirm under the proviso; delay and long custody matter (*Manish Sisodia*, 2024; *Prem Prakash*, 2024) |
| SC/ST (PoA) Act s.18, 18A | Anticipatory bail barred | Bar does not apply where no prima facie case under the Act is made out (*Prathvi Raj Chauhan*, 2020) |
| POCSO Act | No separate bail test, but fifteen-day notice to the Public Prosecutor under the proviso to s.483(1) and the informant's obligatory presence under s.483(2) for BNS s.65/70(2) offences, gravity, child victim's safety | Juvenile accused: JJ Act s.12 |
| Juvenile Justice Act s.12 | Bail of a child is the rule, refused only if release would bring the child into association with criminals, expose the child to danger or defeat the ends of justice | Age determination first |
| Companies Act s.212(6) | Twin conditions for SFIO-investigated fraud offences | Category C in *Antil* |

## 6. Default bail (s.187(3) / 167(2) proviso) — the mechanics

- Period: **90 days** where the offence is punishable with death, imprisonment for life, or imprisonment **for a term of not less than ten years** (minimum ten — *Rakesh Kumar Paul*); **60 days** for any other offence. Special statutes above.
- Runs from the date of **first remand**, counting that day (*Kapil Wadhawan*, 2024 INSC 58, resolving the earlier conflict).
- The right accrues on expiry and is "indefeasible" only while exercised: the accused must apply and be prepared to furnish bail **before** the charge sheet is filed (*Sanjay Dutt*; *M. Ravindran*; *Bikramjit Singh*). An incomplete or "preliminary" charge sheet filed to defeat the right does not count (*Ritu Chhabaria*, 2023 — read with the later clarification that pending matters are decided by their own benches).
- Conditions can be imposed, but the right cannot be denied on merits.

## 7. Undertrial release (s.479 / 436A) — the mechanics

- Detention during investigation, inquiry or trial for **one-half** of the maximum imprisonment for the offence → release on bond, with or without sureties; **one-third** for a first-time offender (never convicted of any offence). Not for offences punishable with death or life imprisonment. Not where more than one case or more than one offence is pending against the person (s.479(2)).
- The jail superintendent must move the court on completion of the period (s.479(3)).
- The Supreme Court directed on 23 August 2024 (*In re Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons*, W.P.(C) 406/2013) that s.479 applies to all undertrials, including those whose cases were pending on 1 July 2024.
- Separate from this, *In re Policy Strategy for Grant of Bail* (Suo Motu W.P.(Crl.) 4/2021, order of 31 January 2023) directed that an accused who cannot furnish sureties within a week of a bail order be released on modified conditions, and that courts consider local sureties and verification delays. Cite it when sureties are the obstacle.

## 8. Bonds and sureties

- Bond and surety amounts must not be excessive (s.484 / 440); the court can accept a personal bond.
- Conditions must relate to securing attendance and a fair trial; conditions that control the applicant's life beyond that are liable to be struck down (*Frank Vitus*, 2024: no Google-location pin; *Parvez Lokhandwalla*).
- Forfeiture and cancellation: s.491 (446) procedure; cancellation of bail by the High Court or Sessions Court under s.483(3) (439(2)) on supervening circumstances (*Dolat Ram*).
