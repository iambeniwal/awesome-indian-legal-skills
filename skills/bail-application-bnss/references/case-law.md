# Bail — controlling decisions by issue

Citations are given in the form practitioners use. Confirm each on [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/) (newer judgments carry a neutral citation `YYYY INSC NNN`) before putting it in a filing. Holdings are one-line summaries, not quotations.

## Bail is the rule

- **Gudikanti Narasimhulu v. Public Prosecutor, A.P.**, (1978) 1 SCC 240 — the classic statement of bail factors; liberty is the norm.
- **Sanjay Chandra v. CBI**, (2011) 1 SCC 40 — bail is the rule, jail the exception; economic offences are not automatically excluded; refusal must not be punitive.
- **Prasanta Kumar Sarkar v. Ashis Chatterjee**, (2010) 14 SCC 496 — the factors a High Court must consider; a bail order ignoring them is liable to be set aside.
- **Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI**, (2022) 10 SCC 51 — categories A–D; no custody at charge-sheet stage for cooperating accused in Category A; timelines for deciding applications; non-compliance with s.41/41A entitles the accused to bail.
- **Manish Sisodia v. Directorate of Enforcement**, 2024 INSC 595 — long incarceration and delay in trial justify bail even under PMLA s.45; trial courts and High Courts should not play safe by refusing bail.

## Arrest, notice and grounds of arrest

- **Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar**, (2014) 8 SCC 273 — for offences punishable up to seven years, no routine arrest; the s.41(1)(b) checklist and s.41A notice (now s.35 and s.35(3) BNSS); Magistrates must verify compliance before remand.
- **Md. Asfak Alam v. State of Jharkhand**, 2023 INSC 660 — *Arnesh Kumar* reiterated; disciplinary consequences for non-compliance.
- **Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India**, 2023 INSC 866 — grounds of arrest under PMLA must be furnished in writing.
- **Prabir Purkayastha v. State (NCT of Delhi)**, 2024 INSC 414 — the *Pankaj Bansal* requirement applies to UAPA; "reasons for arrest" are not "grounds of arrest".
- **Vihaan Kumar v. State of Haryana**, 2025 INSC 162 — written grounds of arrest under Article 22(1) are mandatory for every arrest; non-compliance vitiates the arrest and remand.
- **Joginder Kumar v. State of U.P.**, (1994) 4 SCC 260 and **D.K. Basu v. State of W.B.**, (1997) 1 SCC 416 — arrest and custody safeguards.

## Anticipatory bail

- **Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v. State of Punjab**, (1980) 2 SCC 565 — s.438 is to be construed liberally; no inflexible conditions; no requirement of a time limit.
- **Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi)**, (2020) 5 SCC 1 (Constitution Bench) — anticipatory bail need not be limited in time and can continue till the end of trial, subject to the court's discretion on the facts.
- **Siddharam Satlingappa Mhetre v. State of Maharashtra**, (2011) 1 SCC 694 — factors for anticipatory bail; personal liberty emphasised.
- **Lavesh v. State (NCT of Delhi)**, (2012) 8 SCC 730 — a proclaimed absconder is normally not entitled to anticipatory bail.
- **Srikant Upadhyay v. State of Bihar**, 2024 INSC 202 — pendency of an anticipatory-bail application does not bar proclamation; an accused who evades process cannot expect the extraordinary relief.
- **Prathvi Raj Chauhan v. Union of India**, (2020) 4 SCC 727 — the SC/ST Act bar on anticipatory bail is valid but does not apply where no prima facie case under the Act is made out.

## Default bail

- **Sanjay Dutt v. State through CBI, Bombay (II)**, (1994) 5 SCC 410 (Constitution Bench) — the right to default bail is enforceable only until the charge sheet is filed; once exercised it is indefeasible.
- **Uday Mohanlal Acharya v. State of Maharashtra**, (2001) 5 SCC 453 — "availing of" the right means filing the application and offering bail; the court cannot defeat it by delay.
- **Rakesh Kumar Paul v. State of Assam**, (2017) 15 SCC 67 — "not less than ten years" means a minimum of ten; offences punishable "up to ten years" attract the 60-day period.
- **M. Ravindran v. Intelligence Officer, DRI**, (2021) 2 SCC 485 — the right is a facet of Article 21; a subsequent charge sheet does not defeat an application already filed.
- **Bikramjit Singh v. State of Punjab**, (2020) 10 SCC 616 — only the court with jurisdiction over the offence (Special Court under UAPA) can extend the period; default bail is a fundamental right.
- **Enforcement Directorate v. Kapil Wadhawan**, 2024 INSC 58 — the day of first remand is included in computing the 60/90 days.
- **Ritu Chhabaria v. Union of India**, 2023 INSC 426 — a charge sheet filed without completing investigation cannot defeat default bail (note the later order that pending cases be decided independently; check status before relying).
- **Judgebir Singh v. National Investigation Agency**, 2023 INSC 525 — default bail under UAPA: extension orders must be on the Public Prosecutor's report and the accused must be heard.

## Special statutes

- **Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb**, (2021) 3 SCC 713 — UAPA s.43D(5) does not oust constitutional courts' power to grant bail where incarceration is prolonged and trial is not in sight.
- **NIA v. Zahoor Ahmad Shah Watali**, (2019) 5 SCC 1 — under s.43D(5), the court looks at whether the accusation is prima facie true on the material, without a mini-trial.
- **Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India**, 2022 SCC OnLine SC 929 — PMLA s.45 twin conditions upheld.
- **Mohd. Muslim v. State (NCT of Delhi)**, 2023 INSC 311 — NDPS s.37: long incarceration and no prospect of early trial weigh in favour of bail; the test is "reasonable grounds", not a finding of innocence.
- **Prem Prakash v. Union of India**, 2024 INSC 637 — PMLA: liberty is the rule even under s.45; statements to ED officers while in custody in another case are inadmissible.
- **P. Chidambaram v. Directorate of Enforcement**, (2020) 13 SCC 791 — gravity of an economic offence is a relevant factor but not an absolute bar; custody cannot be insisted upon merely to deter.

## Undertrials, sureties and conditions

- **In re Inhuman Conditions in 1382 Prisons**, W.P.(C) 406/2013, order of 23 August 2024 — BNSS s.479 applies retrospectively to all undertrials; jail superintendents to move applications.
- **In re Policy Strategy for Grant of Bail**, Suo Motu W.P.(Crl.) 4/2021, order of 31 January 2023 — release on modified conditions where sureties cannot be furnished within a week; verification delays not to keep the accused in custody.
- **Hussainara Khatoon v. Home Secretary, State of Bihar**, (1980) 1 SCC 81 — speedy trial is part of Article 21; undertrials cannot be held beyond the sentence they could receive.
- **Frank Vitus v. Narcotics Control Bureau**, 2024 INSC 479 — bail conditions must not be arbitrary; no requirement to share live location or obtain embassy certificates in every case.
- **Parvez Noordin Lokhandwalla v. State of Maharashtra**, (2020) 10 SCC 77 — conditions must be proportionate; a condition preventing travel abroad for work was relaxed.
- **Moti Ram v. State of M.P.**, (1978) 4 SCC 47 — excessive bail amounts and insistence on local sureties are impermissible.

## Cancellation and parity

- **Dolat Ram v. State of Haryana**, (1995) 1 SCC 349 — cancellation requires supervening circumstances (interference, abuse of liberty, flight), not a re-hearing of merits.
- **Puran v. Rambilas**, (2001) 6 SCC 338 — a bail order ignoring relevant material or granted perversely can be set aside by a superior court; distinct from cancellation for conduct.
- **Ramesh Bhavan Rathod v. Vishanbhai Hirabhai Makwana**, (2021) 6 SCC 230 — parity is not mechanical; the court must consider each accused's role.
- **Mahipal v. Rajesh Kumar**, (2020) 2 SCC 118 — grant of bail without reasons is unsustainable.
