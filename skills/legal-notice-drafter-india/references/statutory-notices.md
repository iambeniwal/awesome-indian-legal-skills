# Statutory notices in Indian practice

Verify every period and content requirement on [India Code](https://www.indiacode.gov.in/) before drafting — amendments and State variations exist. Decisions: confirm on [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/). Holdings are one-line summaries.

## General rules that apply across the table

- **Deemed service**: s.27 General Clauses Act — a document sent by properly addressed, prepaid registered post is deemed served when it would arrive in the ordinary course, unless the contrary is proved. Keep the receipt and tracking.
- **No general precondition**: an ordinary civil suit between private parties needs no notice. A tactical notice still helps — it fixes the demand, the date and the recipient's position, and courts read an unanswered notice against the recipient.
- **Limitation runs regardless** of a notice unless the statute excludes the notice period (s.80 CPC via s.15(2) Limitation Act; s.12A(3) Commercial Courts Act) or fixes a commencement date (s.21 Arbitration).
- **Criminal intimidation and extortion** (BNS ss.351, 308): a notice that threatens criminal proceedings, police complaints or exposure unless money is paid can itself be an offence and professional misconduct. State lawful remedies only.
- **Defamation** (BNS s.356; civil action): statements in a notice are not privileged merely because an advocate signs them. Keep to documented facts.

## Decisions

- **State of Punjab v. Geeta Iron & Brass Works Ltd**, (1978) 1 SCC 68 — s.80 notice is meant to give the Government a chance to settle; the Government should not take technical pleas but the notice is still required.
- **Bihari Chowdhary v. State of Bihar**, (1984) 2 SCC 627 — s.80 is mandatory; a suit filed before the two months expire is not maintainable.
- **Patil Automation Pvt Ltd v. Rakheja Engineers Pvt Ltd**, 17 August 2022 ((2022) 10 SCC 1) — s.12A is mandatory; plaints filed without pre-institution mediation (and not seeking urgent interim relief) are to be rejected, prospectively from 20 August 2022.
- **Yamini Manohar v. T.K.D. Keerthi**, 2023 INSC 943 — the urgent-interim-relief exception is judged on the plaint as framed; the court checks that the claim for urgency is not a camouflage.
- **Mobilox Innovations Pvt Ltd v. Kirusa Software Pvt Ltd**, (2018) 1 SCC 353 — a pre-existing dispute that is plausible and not spurious defeats an operational creditor's petition.
- **Mardia Chemicals Ltd v. Union of India**, (2004) 4 SCC 311 — SARFAESI upheld; the borrower's objections must be considered and answered with reasons (leading to s.13(3A)).
- **Bharat Sanchar Nigam Ltd v. Nortel Networks India Pvt Ltd**, (2021) 5 SCC 738 — a s.11 petition is governed by Article 137 Limitation Act: three years from refusal to appoint after a s.21 notice.
- **Arif Azim Co. Ltd v. Aptech Ltd**, 2024 INSC 155 — limitation for s.11 and the court's limited look at whether the claims are ex facie time-barred.
- **Silpi Industries v. Kerala State Road Transport Corporation**, (2021) 18 SCC 790 — MSMED registration must precede the supply for the Act's benefits; counter-claims are maintainable before the Council.
- **Gujarat State Civil Supplies Corporation v. Mahakali Foods Pvt Ltd**, 2022 INSC 1116 — the MSMED Act's Chapter V overrides an independent arbitration clause.
- **Kross Television India Pvt Ltd v. Vikas Sahni**, 2017 SCC OnLine Bom 3086 — WhatsApp/email service accepted where the defendant was evading (supplementary, not primary).
