# RTI Act, 2005 — controlling decisions by issue

Citations are in the reporter form most practitioners use. Confirm each on [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/) (newer judgments carry a neutral citation `YYYY INSC NNN`) before putting it in a filing. Holdings are one-line summaries, not quotations.

## What is a "public authority" (s.2(h))

- **Thalappalam Service Cooperative Bank Ltd v. State of Kerala**, (2013) 16 SCC 82 — cooperative societies are not public authorities merely because a Registrar regulates them; "substantially financed" means funding of a substantial nature, not any grant; the burden is on the applicant/Commission to show it.
- **CPIO, Supreme Court of India v. Subhash Chandra Agarwal**, 2019 INSC 1265, (2020) 5 SCC 481 — the office of the Chief Justice of India is a public authority; judges' asset declarations held by the CJI are "information"; disclosure of personal information turns on a public-interest balancing (now read with the DPDP amendment to s.8(1)(j)).
- **Indian Olympic Association v. Veeresh Malik**, Delhi HC 2010 — sports bodies substantially financed by the Government are public authorities (High Court; persuasive).

## What is "information" and what a PIO need not do

- **CBSE v. Aditya Bandopadhyay**, (2011) 8 SCC 497 — evaluated answer sheets are information and not held in a fiduciary relationship; an applicant need not give reasons; a PIO is not required to collate, create, interpret, or give opinions; the Act is not meant to divert resources disproportionately.
- **Khanapuram Gandaiah v. Administrative Officer**, (2010) 2 SCC 1 — a judge cannot be asked under RTI why a particular decision was reached; reasons and opinions are not "information".
- **Institute of Chartered Accountants of India v. Shaunak H. Satya**, (2011) 8 SCC 781 — instructions and model answers given to examiners are exempt under s.8(1)(d)/(e) until the process is over; the fiduciary exemption protects the examiner-institute relationship, not the candidate's own evaluated sheet.
- **UPSC v. Angesh Kumar**, (2018) 4 SCC 530 — raw marks, cut-offs and scaling details of a competitive exam may be withheld where disclosure would compromise the system; the Commission must weigh the public interest.

## Exemptions

- **Girish Ramchandra Deshpande v. Central Information Commission**, (2013) 1 SCC 212 — a public servant's service record, assets, income-tax returns and disciplinary details are personal information under s.8(1)(j); exempt unless the applicant shows a larger public interest. *Note: s.44(3) DPDP Act, 2023 amends s.8(1)(j) to drop the public-interest qualifier — check commencement.*
- **Bihar Public Service Commission v. Saiyed Hussain Abbas Rizwi**, (2012) 13 SCC 61 — names and addresses of interview-board members are exempt under s.8(1)(g)/(j); the public interest in disclosure must be weighed against the harm.
- **Reserve Bank of India v. Jayantilal N. Mistry**, (2016) 3 SCC 525 — RBI inspection reports and information about banks are not held in a fiduciary capacity for the banks; disclosable; later clarificatory proceedings should be checked.
- **Central Public Information Officer, Supreme Court v. Subhash Chandra Agarwal** (above) — s.8(1)(e) fiduciary and s.8(1)(j) privacy analysed; proportionality applies.
- **Union of India v. Central Information Commission** (Delhi HC, several matters) — High Court decisions on Cabinet papers (s.8(1)(i)) and file notings; file notings are generally "information" (DoPT's own position since 2009).

## Procedure, fees and the appeal/complaint distinction

- **Chief Information Commissioner v. State of Manipur**, (2011) 15 SCC 1 — on a s.18 complaint the Commission can inquire and penalise but cannot direct disclosure; the route to get information is the s.19 appeal.
- **Namit Sharma v. Union of India**, (2013) 1 SCC 745, reviewed in **Union of India v. Namit Sharma**, (2013) 10 SCC 359 — Commissions are not courts; no requirement that benches include judicial members; eligibility of Commissioners.
- **Anjali Bhardwaj v. Union of India**, (2019) 18 SCC 246 — directions to fill Commission vacancies in time and to make appointments transparent; useful when a Commission is non-functional.
- **Registrar, Supreme Court of India v. R.S. Misra**, Delhi HC 2017 — RTI cannot be used to re-agitate a decided judicial matter or to seek reasons for judicial orders (High Court).

## Penalties

- s.20(1): ₹250 per day of delay, capped at ₹25,000, on the PIO personally, where the refusal or delay was without reasonable cause, malafide, or the information was incorrect/incomplete; the PIO must be heard first. s.20(2): recommendation for disciplinary action. Commissions impose penalties sparingly; plead specific facts (dates, reminders, the FAA's order being ignored).
