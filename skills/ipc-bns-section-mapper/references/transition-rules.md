# Transition rules: which code governs

The three new laws came into force on **1 July 2024** (S.O. 850(E), 851(E), 852(E) of 23 February 2024). The repealed Acts: IPC 1860 (by BNS s.358), CrPC 1973 (by BNSS s.531), Indian Evidence Act 1872 (by BSA s.170). Read the savings clauses on India Code before applying anything below; High Court positions on edge cases continued to develop through 2025–26.

## The two-axis rule

| Axis | Governing principle | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Substantive offence and punishment** | Law in force on the **date of the act**. No conviction under a law not in force at the time; no penalty greater than the one then applicable. | Article 20(1), Constitution; BNS s.358 read with s.6 General Clauses Act |
| **Procedure** (investigation, arrest, remand, bail, trial, appeal, revision) | Proceedings **pending** on 1 July 2024 continue under the CrPC. Proceedings **instituted** on or after 1 July 2024 follow the BNSS, whatever the date of the offence. | BNSS s.531(2)(a) |
| **Evidence** | Same pattern: pending proceedings under the IEA; new ones under the BSA. | BSA s.170(2) |

"Pending" has been read in the ordinary sense: an FIR registered, a complaint filed, an appeal or revision instituted before 1 July 2024. A fresh step inside an old proceeding (a bail application in a 2023 FIR, filed in 2025) is generally treated as part of the pending proceeding and governed by the CrPC — but several High Courts have let later applications cite the BNSS provision as well, and the point is not uniformly settled. State the position of the user's High Court or flag the uncertainty.

## Worked examples

1. **Assault in March 2024, FIR registered in August 2024.** Offence: IPC s.323 (BNS does not apply to the act). Procedure: BNSS — investigation under s.173 ff., remand under s.187, bail under s.480/483, charge sheet under s.193. The FIR should read "u/s 323 IPC" and the bail application cites the BNSS.
2. **Cheating in September 2024, FIR in October 2024.** Offence: BNS s.318(4). Procedure: BNSS. Evidence: BSA (electronic records under s.63 with the Schedule certificate).
3. **FIR registered in May 2024, charge sheet filed in September 2024.** Offence: IPC. Procedure: CrPC throughout (pending on 1 July) — charge sheet under CrPC s.173, trial under CrPC, appeal provisions of CrPC. Some courts have applied BNSS timelines to later stages; check locally.
4. **Continuing offence (e.g. cruelty under IPC s.498A/BNS s.85) spanning the date.** Charge acts before 1 July under IPC s.498A and acts on/after under BNS s.85, or frame the charge to make the dates explicit; do not charge pre-July acts under the BNS.
5. **Appeal filed in 2025 against a 2023 conviction after a CrPC trial.** Appeal provisions: the better view is that the appeal is a continuation of the pending proceeding and CrPC applies, but High Courts have split. Cite both and let the court decide; the substantive law is unaffected either way.
6. **Beneficial change in punishment.** Where the BNS prescribes a lighter punishment (or adds community service) for an offence committed under the IPC, Article 20(1) does not itself entitle the accused to the lighter sentence, but courts may consider the legislative policy at sentencing. Flag as an argument, not a right.

## Points to check every time

- **Commencement of specific provisions**: BNS s.106(2) (hit-and-run) was held back by the Government after the January 2024 transporter protests; confirm on e-Gazette whether and when it was brought into force.
- **Cognizability and bailability** follow the First Schedule of the **BNSS** for BNS offences and the First Schedule of the CrPC (as saved) for IPC offences in pending proceedings. Punishment ranges differ for some offences, which can move an offence across the bail line — check the schedule, not just the section number.
- **Limitation for cognizance** (CrPC s.468 / BNSS s.514): computed from the date of the offence; the applicable code follows the procedure axis.
- **Sanction provisions** (CrPC s.197 / BNSS s.218) and **compounding** (CrPC s.320 / BNSS s.359): the BNSS table of compoundable offences lists BNS sections; for an IPC offence in a BNSS proceeding, courts have matched the corresponding BNS entry.
- **Electronic evidence**: a BSA s.63 certificate (Schedule format) in a BSA proceeding; an IEA s.65B certificate in an IEA proceeding. Mixing them is a common error.
- **Erroneous section numbers in an FIR or charge sheet** do not by themselves vitiate proceedings — the court looks at the facts alleged — but they affect bail arguments, the applicable schedule and punishment, so point them out and suggest correction under BNSS s.193(9)/supplementary report or at charge-framing.

## Where the numbering traps are

- The same number means different things across codes: s.173 is the charge-sheet section in the CrPC but the FIR section in the BNSS; s.482 is quashing in the CrPC but anticipatory bail in the BNSS; s.144 is the prohibitory-order section in the CrPC but maintenance in the BNSS.
- BNS sub-sections: many IPC offences became sub-sections of a single BNS section (theft s.303, cheating s.318, CBT s.316, forgery s.336). Cite the sub-section, not just the section.
- Omitted provisions: IPC s.309, s.377, s.497, and sedition in its s.124A form. Do not map these to a "nearest" section without saying it is not an equivalent.
